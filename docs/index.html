Bend-UI

Bend-UI takes advantage of Bend's Laws to make design rules enforceable at compile time. Agents can generate screens, create components, and expand the library without compromising consistency.

Move fast without design drift. Let agents build freely while Laws enforce the same spacing, sizing, radii, and interaction rules across every component.

Accessibility

Accessibility isn't something anyone turns on. A blind person can't find an "accessibility mode", so the library doesn't have one. Instead it works in three layers.

1. Always on: WCAG 2.2 AA

Every component is built to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA by default, with no option to turn it off: text and line contrast, roles and names for screen readers, everything usable from the keyboard, a visible focus, and changes announced as they happen. People who don't need it don't notice it.

2. Automatic: the person's own settings

People who need more set it once on their computer or phone, and the library follows: reduced motion (prefers-reduced-motion), more contrast (prefers-contrast), forced colors (Windows high contrast) and text zoom. No setting lives in the app.

3. Your choice: a larger size

The library is drawn at its minimum. An app can choose a larger size scale, and an app can offer it to its own users as a "comfortable" size. Everything grows through the tokens, and the Laws still hold at every size. WCAG asks for targets of at least 24px at AA and 44px at AAA.

Components are reviewed one at a time. Each component's page lists every WCAG criterion it touches and where it stands on each one. A page carries the WCAG conformance logo only after it passes every A and AA criterion and has been tested with a keyboard and with screen readers (VoiceOver and NVDA).
